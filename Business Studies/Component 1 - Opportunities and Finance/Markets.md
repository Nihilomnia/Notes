## Types of markets

### Local markets
Local markets focus on **one single area** (e.g. it's own city, town, geographic area) and are often small with a limited number of customers and lower levels of sales and profit.

#### Advantages
1. Greater understanding of the culture of their customer base in their community (wants, needs, beliefs and values etc.)
2. Cultural relevance - supporting local events, building community trust, highlighting region-specific issues, campaigns that resonate with area etc.
3. Tighter audience targeting.


#### Challenges 
1. Limited reach: local market will become saturated
2. Dependence on local trends and economies. If the local economy slows or preferences in the area shift rapidly, the business could suffer.
3. Competition from larger corporation (especially price). They potentially mitigate this by prioritizing customer service and emphasis on supporting local.

### Global Markets 

Global markets are **international markets** that are large, often with multinational business selling products to many different countries.

#### Advantages 
1. Much wider reach with greater potential for growth, Expanding globally allows the business to tap into new, potentially lucrative markets.
2. Economies of scale: scaling up operation leads to greater efficiency.
3. Increased brand visibility: globally recognizable brands can add prestige and trust.

#### Challenges 
1. Cultural sensitivity: operating in many different cultural,social,and even political contexts can be tricky to negotiate
2. Logistical complexity: language barriers, regulatory differences, varying consumer behaviors, complex distribution network networks.
3. Fierce competition now up against international giants. Standing out in a crowed global market is difficult.  


### Niche Market 
Where a business targets a smaller segment of a larger market, where customers have specific needs and wants.

#### Advantages 
1. **Reduced competition** due to how specific the market is.
2. **Clear focus** makes it easy to target particular customer
3. Builds up specialist skill and knowledge which leads to **market expertise**.
4. **Higher prices** potentially - customers are prepared to pay for specialty experience
5. **Profit margins** often are higher.
6. Customer tend to be more **loyal**.
#### Disadvantages
1. Lack of **economies of scale**
2. Risk of **over-dependence** on a single product or market.
3. Likely to attract **competition** if successful
4. Vulnerable to markets change changes - all their eggs are in one basket!
### Mass Market 
Where a business sells into the largest part of the market, where there are many similar products on offer.

The business offers products at a large markets, trying to attract the highest number of potential customers rather than particular customers.

Similar goods are produced in large quantities, ignoring market segments and there are attempts to appeal to the entire market.


#### Advantages
1. **Larger customer base**: With a mass markets, businesses have access to a large customer base, which can result in **higher sales and profits**.
2. **Economies of scale**: Mass markets products and services can be produced and distributed at a larger scale, resulting on lower production costs and higher profit margins.
3. **Brand Recognition**: By appealing to a broad audience, mass market products and services can build strong brand recognition and customer loyalty.


#### Disadvantages
1. **More competition**: With a large customer base comes more competition from other business striving for the same customers.
2. **Less personalisation**: Mass market products and services are designed to appeal to a broad audience, which means they **not meet the specific needs and wants** of individual customers.
3. **Lower profits margins**: While mass markets products may sell in larger volumes, the profits margins may lower due to the need to keep prices affordable.

### Monopolistic Competition

The situation in a market in which elements of a [Monopoly](<#Monopoly>) allow individuals producers or consumers to exercise some control over market prices.

Examples : Restaurants, Hairdressing and Clothing Industry 

Characteristics of Monopolistic Competition 
1. **Many buyers/sellers** - There are numerous firms in the market, each producing slightly differentiated products.
   
2. **Product Differentiation** - Each firm produces a product that is similar but not identical.
   
3. **Ease of Entry and Exit** - Firms can enter or exit the market relatively easily 
   
4. **Non-Price Competition** - Firms engage in non price competition to attract customers. This includes advertising, product design, branding and customer service.
   
5. **Limited Price Control** - While firms have some degree of pricing power due to product differentiation, they face competition from other firms offering similar products. As a result, they cannot significantly raise prices without losing customers .
   
6. **Short-Run and Long-Run** profits - In the short run, firms may earn economic profits or incur losses.  In the long run, however, economic profits tend to be eroded.
   
7. **Imperfect Information** - Consumers may not have perfect information about all available products making advertising and branding important tools for firms to differentiate their products.

Pricing strategies used in Competitive oligopolies  

1. Price Wars - continuously lowering prices to undercut each other // This is generally avoided.

3. Limit Pricing - The Dominant firm setting low prices to discourage new entrants into the market.
   
Generally though, we see oligopolies feature price rigidity.

There are incentives to keep prices stables, deriving from the interdependence between firms.


#### Collusive Oligopolies 

With oligopoly market structures comes uncertainty. To reduce this, firms could choose to collude with each other - perhaps in the form of a cartel agreement or a price ring to fix prices or maybe to restrict output.

Cartel agreements to fix prices allow efficient firms to make abnormal profit, and inefficient firms to stay in business.

Collusive oligopolies therefore present many of the disadvantages founds in a monopoly, but without the benefits of economies of scale and improvements in efficiency/ R&D.

PED > 1 --> Elastic  --> Significant Change in demand

When something is price elastic is demand, the quantity demanded would be more responsive to changes in price - Such as Bread and Ham.

Factors that influence price elasticity of demand
#### Factors that influence PED

- Sustainability: Products with readily available substitutes tend to have higher elasticity because consumers can easily switch to alternatives if the price rises. For instance, if once brand of cereal became overly expensive, there are plenty more.
  
- Necessity vs. Luxury: Necessities often have inelastic demand because consumers need them regardless of price changes. luxuries have more elastic demand.
  
- Proportion of Income: Expensive items that take up a significant portion of a consumer income (e.g. holidays and luxury items) typically have more elastic demand. If the economy takes a downward turn more people would abandon these types of products.
  
- Brand Loyalty : Strong brand loyalty can make demand more inelastic, as loyal customers may be less sensitive to price changes.

### Income Elasticity of Demand  (YED)
As incomes rise quantity demanded will rise e.g practically everything else. A inferior good something deemed to be lower quality if income rose your quantity of demand will fall --> E.g Public Transport, Discount Stores, (Fast Food)

YED measures the responsiveness of demand to a change income.

Income Elastic Demand - When demand is highly and positively responsive to income change.

Income Inelastic Demand - When demand only responds a little to a change in income.

YED = % Change in quantity demanded / % change in Income 


$$
\text{YED} = \frac{\%\Delta QD}{\%\Delta I}
$$



Normal Goods  --> YED > 0 
Luxury good --> YED  > 1 
Inferior good --> YED < 0 

YED is influenced by many factors in an economy that changes the wage of workers.

During a recession, wages usually fall and demand for Inferior goods rise while demand for luxury goods fall

During a period of economic growth  and rising wages demand for luxury goods will increase while those that are inferior will decrease.

Other influences on income include minimum wage legislation, taxation and increased international trade.

### Market Share

Market Share is a measure of the percentage share that an individual business holds of the overall market  that they operating in.

The higher their market share, the more power and influence that they hold and the greater their ability to influence the market price .

Formula: 

$$\text{Market Share} = \left( \frac{\text{Sales of Business}}{\text{Total Market Sales}} \right) \times 100$$



#### Why is it important 

- Market Leader  - Business with the highest share are often called the market leader this gives them more power 
- Economies of Scale - Higher volume sales often lad to lower average costs
- Objective setting - A common corporate is to increase market share even if the market size itself is falling.
